# Pioneer Recruitment Spec — Door-to-Door Cohort of 10

**Status:** draft (v0.1 — field-recruitment runbook. Builds on
`REAL_RETAILER_ONBOARDING_SPEC.md` Phase-1 concierge posture and
`MARKET_LAUNCH_PLAYBOOK_SPEC.md`; invents no machinery — it wraps existing
surfaces in a field workflow).

**Note:** §1 (the offer, word-of-mouth, operator pitch, stickiness, moat)
doubles as the **shared narrative spine** for the door pitch, the
community-partner pitch, and the investor pitch — pull from here rather
than fork the story into separate decks.

**Purpose:** Recruit the pioneer cohort — **10 real, claimed retailers** —
by walking into stores. Cross-category by design: each pioneer targets a
category with **no live shelf**, so 10 pioneers ≈ 10 new `/place/category/*`
shelves. The cohort is the proof set — claimed, verified, indexed, shoppable.

**Why field recruitment:** it is the only channel that *produces* the
strongest verification evidence as a side effect. A storefront photo taken
inside the store is the top `physical` signal class
(`CLAIM_VERIFICATION_SIGNALS_SPEC` §1.1) — recruitment and verification
happen in the same visit.

---

## 1. Cohort definition

A business counts as a pioneer when ALL of:

- Seed or listing claimed (`org_standing_mode` → `independent`)
- Storefront live at its canonical URL (`/tenant/{id}` / subdomain / domain)
- ≥1 public product or a complete shelf card (NAP + hours + category + photo)
- Indexed or in the sitemap discovery pipeline (automatic — verify, don't fix)
- Storefront photo captured in person (verification evidence)

**Paid conversion is NOT the pioneer bar.** The cohort proves the shelf
works; the upgrade triad works it afterward. Target: 10 pioneers, ≥5
distinct categories, ≥3 with no prior live shelf.

**Sizing the door list:** decision-maker present ≈ 1 in 3 doors.
10 pioneers ⇒ ~40–60 doors ⇒ 2–3 walk days on dense corridors.

**Market:** Indianapolis. Home market, existing indexed seed depth (80
`/place/*` seeds incl. Enerem — already in Google results), claim precedent
(Global Grace Grocery), walkable retail corridors.

### 1.1 The offer is pioneer status — not a discount

The pitch is explicit: **founding members of the platform.** The retailer
isn't buying a listing — they're being recruited as one of ten businesses
that *shape what this becomes*. Concretely:

- **Product input** — pioneers' feedback routes to the roadmap; their
  category pain points become the next scans, their workflow gaps the next
  features. Said plainly on the door and meant literally: the platform
  exists to be shaped by its first real retailers.
- **Growth partnership** — their shelf gets indexed, their category page
  gets built around *their* business first, and the platform's growth next
  year is their shelf traffic growing. The pitch stakes the platform's
  roadmap on their growth targets, not the reverse.
- **Initially free, never "free."** The claim is free — listing live,
  storefront up, indexed — but the offer is explicitly a **growth
  partnership**, not a free offer. They start free and grow *with* the
  platform: as capabilities unlock value (products, BOPIS, QR, coupons,
  CRM, chatbot), the ladder is the shared journey — presence → discovery →
  storefront → paid presence modes. The pioneer isn't getting something for
  nothing; they're entering a partnership where the platform's growth and
  theirs compound on each other. Free is the door; partnership is the deal.
- **Scarcity is real** — ten slots, named cohort. Not artificial pressure;
  the cohort genuinely closes.

This replaces price-led framing entirely. The door line is closer to
*"we're recruiting the first ten stores this platform is built around —
yours is already listed on it"* than anything about cost.

### 1.2 The word-of-mouth design (the retailer's inner monologue)

The pitch is built so the retailer tells the story *for* us. The hoped-for
internal monologue:

> "This platform is recruiting *me* to be one of the first on their
> storefront platform. I've been thinking about building a website for my
> business — and this just arrived to make it real. It's like the prayer
> got answered."

Two properties make that monologue likely rather than hopeful:

- **Arrival timing.** The pitch doesn't create a desire — it answers an
  *existing* one. Independent retailers have wanted a web presence for
  years; the barrier was cost, skill, and time. "We already built your
  page, claim it" lands on a wish they already had.
- **The story flatters the teller.** "They recruited me to be first" is a
  story the owner *wants* to repeat — it makes them the protagonist. Every
  pioneer knows a dozen other owners; the cohort seeds its own referrals,
  and the next market's recruitment starts warm.

**Mechanic:** make the referral path explicit, not hoped-for. At claim
success, ask: *"Know another store that should be on here?"* — one tap,
referral captured, candidate feeds the discovery queue. The pioneer cohort
becomes the sourcing channel for the *next* cohort.

### 1.3 The testimony layer — discoverability hooks, capability testifies

The pitch and the testimony are different stories:

- **The door pitch is discoverability** — "you're already listed, already
  findable on Google." Simple, proven (today), credible to a stranger.
- **The testimony is capability** — *"I tried it and it does more than I
  imagined":* BOPIS, QR codes, coupons, funnels, growing into a real sales
  channel, selling online *and* offline — and behind that, the
  capability-gated growth stack other platforms only promise: a chatbot on
  their storefront, a CRM, FAQ management, anonymous shopper inquiries that
  land in a real requests hub. The recruit arrives for the listing; they
  evangelize for the toolkit.

Design consequence: the claim success moment and the first-week experience
should **reveal capability progressively** — each surfaced feature (QR kit,
coupon, BOPIS toggle) is a sentence in the testimony they'll tell. The
upgrade triad isn't a paywall gate in this frame — it's the "there's more"
reveal.

**Pitch angles — per-prospect, from the signals.** The door pitch picks
the angle the prospect's record already suggests — the scans detect them:

- **Dead/missing Google website link** — the GBP website field is empty,
  hijacked, parked, or expired (existing listing-drift/missing-site
  signals). Each triptych door is a live answer to that dead click — the
  same storefront on the platform path, subdomain, or custom domain, *with
  their branding*: "your Google profile points at nothing — here's the
  website it should have had, already built."
- **No profile at all** — invisible business: "you're not on Google; your
  listing here is already live."
- **Strong reviews, no web presence** — "4.9 stars and customers still
  can't see what you sell."
- **The directory is a presence channel Google can't match** — a Google
  listing is a point; the directory exposes *all eligible category shelves*
  on the seed's own surface AND places the business on each shelf's page.
  Bidirectional shelf membership: the seed shows which shelves it qualifies
  for, each shelf page carries the seed. "Google shows you one dot on a
  map — we put you on every shelf you belong on."
- **The $2,500 anchor** — a branded agency website runs $2,500+ before
  hosting, and most of these owners have already been quoted and declined
  (that's why the scans keep finding missing/parked/hijacked domains).
  The pitch: "a real branded storefront usually costs thousands — yours is
  already live, and claiming it costs nothing." The scans surface the
  *evidence* (the dead link) and the *objection killer* (the price) in the
  same record.
- **BOPIS — the store is the fulfillment center** — "order online, pick up
  in store" turns their existing shelves into a fulfillment node; the big
  competitors already run it, and the platform's BOPIS machinery gives an
  independent the same motion without a delivery fleet. The pickup probe
  from the call spec doubles as the opener: "a customer asks if they can
  order {product} online and pick it up today — right now the answer is
  no."
- **Chain/multi-location** — "each location gets its own storefront" (chain
  feed already groups them).
- **Manager present, owner absent** — the §1.4 operator framing.

The angle is chosen per door from the seed/queue record — the pitch is
templated, not improvised.

### 1.45 The two-act recruitment arc

The whole motion is two acts, and act 1 already contains a taste of act 2:

- **Act 1 — the seed (free, already live):** the category-channel listing
  exists before the retailer ever signs up. Discoverable, indexed,
  shelf-member — presence for free, no action needed. This is the opening
  act of recruitment: *"you're already on it."*
- **The free taste (the bridge):** claiming unlocks the free 5-product
  slots — the storefront's minimum working set. Five products is enough to
  feel the storefront working — indexed items, shopper views, pickup
  asks — and exactly small enough to outgrow. **Act 1 ships with a free
  taste of act 2 built in.**
- **Act 2 — the full storefront (the growth path):** more than the free 5
  slots — the complete catalog, BOPIS, coupons, QR kits, custom domain.
  The upgrade isn't sold cold; it's the natural next move for an owner who
  already watched 5 products start working.

The structure matters: nobody is asked to imagine act 2 — they've already
*used* it at 5-slot scale by the time the paid path is offered. The pitch
isn't "buy a storefront," it's "keep going."

### 1.5 The stickiness hypothesis — the partnership compounds into moat

The growth-partnership framing is also the retention model. There is no
contractual lock-in — the switching cost is **accumulated value**:

- **Indexed equity** — their `/place/*` pages, products, and shelf
  membership are indexed and earning search position. Leaving forfeits
  ranking they didn't have to build themselves.
- **Deployed artifacts** — QR kits printed and taped to the door, coupons
  in circulation, BOPIS running, customers in the CRM, inquiries landing
  in the Requests Hub. These are operational dependencies, not features.
- **Verified standing** — claimed + physically verified status is a
  credential earned here; it doesn't port.
- **Depth keeps expanding** — the capability ladder (app store, chatbot,
  advanced tiers, custom domains) means the platform grows *into* their
  business over time. Every module they adopt deepens the entanglement —
  a competitor would have to replace a channel, not a listing.

**Why the moat compounds:** every capability module is *native* — built on
one architecture with tier/capability gating, no bolt-on third-party
plugins. That means each adopted module shares the retailer's own data
(products, customers, inquiries, reviews) instead of syncing across vendor
silos — and each upgrade is a key flip, not a migration. Competitors can
clone a feature; they can't clone the coherence.

**The chatbot is the showcase case.** It isn't a bolt-on or even a
separate product — it's a **capability-aware module on the same
capability architecture that powers the whole platform**: tenant scope,
storefront scope, directory scope, all governed by the same capability
keys. It turns on when the tier unlocks it, scopes itself to the surface
it lives on (the tenant's storefront answers store questions; the
directory surface answers platform questions), and inherits the same
gating, audit, and context plumbing as every other module. A bolt-on bot
scrapes the page and guesses; this one is *context-aware by
construction* — it reads the tenant's live inventory, real hours, BOPIS
state, verification facts, and inquiry history. "Do you carry {product}?" gets a real answer from the
catalog; "can I pick up today?" gets a real answer from the pickup
capability flag.

The awareness net is wider than catalog data — the tenant's **directory
presence itself is a context source**: active promotions, coupons,
featured status, products, FAQ entries, open inquiries, flyer content,
CRM communication history. "Any deals this week?" answers from the coupon
surface; "what do people ask you?" answers from the FAQ/inquiry records;
a returning customer's earlier conversation is in the CRM the bot sits on
top of. The bot doesn't approximate the store — it *is* the store's
data, speaking.

Design consequence: stickiness is earned by *delivery cadence*, not
contracts. The retention plan is the roadmap — keep shipping capability
modules, and each pioneer becomes progressively harder to move.

**Community channels are the multiplier.** In the target communities
(international grocery, halal, beauty), the trusted broadcast surfaces are
institutional — church testimony, mosque announcements, association
meetings. One pioneer testifying *"they built my store online"* in front of
a congregation reaches a room full of candidate owners — and the story is
self-verifying because every listener can pull out a phone and see the
shelf live. Word of mouth here isn't a metaphor; it's a scheduled weekly
gathering.

### 1.4 The operator pitch to ownership — idle staff hours become the channel

Field observation (Dreamcast, Indianapolis): the floor manager was on the
phone much of the visit — real downtime inside paid hours. That observation
resolves the owner's first objection before it's raised:

> "Who has time to run an online store?" → *"Your manager already does —
> during the hours they're on the floor anyway."*

The pitch to ownership: **appoint an existing staff member as the store's
online operator.** Listing products, answering inquiries, managing the
storefront — done during slow-floor hours, at zero marginal labor cost. The
platform becomes the manager's productive channel; the owner gets an online
storefront staffed by labor they're already paying.

This also re-frames who you're recruiting at the door. When the manager —
not the owner — is present, the visit still produces: the storefront photo,
the NAP verification, and the seed of the idea ("you'd be the one running
this") — the manager becomes an internal advocate who sells the owner
*for* you. A staff member who sees a real role for themselves in the pitch
is an ally inside the store.

## 2. Category depth — the agnostic machinery

Intelligence campaigns are **category-agnostic by construction**: the
category-identification act evaluates a business through its public context
and *suggests shelves* — it does not pick from a closed list. The selector
(`DirectoryCategorySelectorAdapter`, used on `/t/{id}/settings/directory`)
merges three sources:

| Source | What it is |
|---|---|
| `platform_categories` | Registered shelves with live `/place/category/*` pages |
| `mkt_service_categories_list` | Analyst-identified labels — registered vocab, shelf-ready |
| Free-text (`allowCreateNew`) | Every category picker is a discovery surface; a new label is valid |

**Consequence for the cohort:** a pioneer in a category with no live shelf
births that shelf at publish. Prefer *analyst-suggested* labels over
free-text when one is close — registered labels carry enrichment
infrastructure (vocabulary blocks, shelf resolution) that a raw new label
lacks until the next scan registers it.

**Pioneer-facing question:** "Which of these describes your store?" — the
primary-category picker is a publish gate (`canPublish` requires it), so the
conversation is mandatory anyway. Let the owner name their own shelf.

## 3. Pre-walk preparation

Per target corridor, before walking:

1. **Run the intelligence stage for candidate categories** — establishment
   profile if none exists for the category (discovery is gated on it), then
   discovery scans to populate the queue.
2. **Pre-seed targets.** Publish seeds for every door on the list — the
   opener *"you're already listed"* only works if it's true. Queue → seed
   path (`from-queue-<entryId>` batch), provenance rows required.
3. **Verify the shelves resolve.** Each target's category should resolve to
   a live `/place/category/{slug}` page on publish (`count > 0` gate);
   brand-new labels become plain-text chips until their first listing —
   pioneers ARE that first listing.
4. **Tier insurance audit (hard gate).** Before the first door, verify at
   least **two tiers are fully armed and tested** — the gateway
   `directory_presence` (what a claim lands on) plus one growth tier
   (e.g. `professional`, already exercised live by the demo tenant).
   Walk every *eligible* capability module per tier — QR, coupons, BOPIS,
   chatbot, FAQ, CRM — on staging AND prod. The demo tenant exposed
   exactly what an under-configured tier looks like: a promised module
   that doesn't render in front of a prospect is a credibility kill
   mid-pitch. A pioneer upgrading on the spot must hit armed keys, not
   stubs.
5. **Field kit:**
   - Phone with the seeds admin + claim surfaces reachable
   - **Business cards** (ordered, arriving ~Oct 31) — the leave-behind +
     invite artifact. Generic QR → **platform root** (not per-target links):
     the card proves the platform is real, the *conversation* points them
     at their own listing. **Direct the post-scan action every time** —
     never assume the recipient knows what to do on the platform root.
     The verbal instruction is part of the pitch, two paths — both prove
     the platform exists and operates:

     - **Seed exists:** *"Scan it — tap **Directory**, search your
       business name — you're already on it. I'll call to walk you
       through claiming it."*
     - **No seed:** *"Scan it — tap **Directory**, see who's already on
       the platform — yours could be next. Be a pioneer."*

     **Confirmed gap (2026-10-24):** the platform root has **no visible
     storefront signal** — no demo, no live-storefront destination, no
     retailer CTA (nav isn't even in the SSR payload). A scanned first-time
     visitor lands on a marketing page that never answers "what does this
     do for my store."

     **Fix = root real-estate, not just a landing page.** Two prominent
     panels on the platform root — display panel + button + destination —
     without cluttering the page:

     - **Directory panel** → the public directory surface (`/directory` /
       `/place`). "See who's already listed" — the social-proof
       destination for both seed and no-seed scans.
     - **Storefront panel** → a live storefront presentation. Use a real
       tenant as the demo — `placeshelf.store` (Place Shelf,
       `tid-25we5qgj`) is already live on all three host forms, and it's a
       **professional-tier tenant** — its capability keys cover most
       modules (QR, coupons, BOPIS-path surfaces, and nearly the full
       capability set), so the demo shows the *complete* storefront, not a
       stripped one. The panel can demo the three host forms **side
       by side**: *"Same store, three doors"* —
       `/tenant/{id}` (platform path) · `place-shelf.visibleshelf.com`
       (platform subdomain) · `placeshelf.store` (custom domain). The
       triptych IS the pitch: it shows the canonical ladder visually —
       start on the platform, grow into your own address. **Each door
       carries its own platform-branded QR** resolving to that host form —
       scan any of the three, the same store appears on the visitor's own
       phone. The wow factor is self-verifying: three codes, one business,
       proven by their own device (QR surfaces + analytics exist —
       `QrAnalyticsService`; brand each code with the platform mark).
       And it changes
       what "pioneer" means to the listener: this is not an MVP asking
       for beta testers — it's a working, fully functional product
       inviting its first members. The sophistication *is* the
       reassurance: they're early, not experimental. "This is what
       your store gets" — the capability proof, worth equal weight
       because the storefront IS the product.

     Plus the `/s/…` short-code → pioneer landing (claim CTA) for the
     card traffic — the landing carries the close; the root panels carry
     the proof. Until they ship, the verbal direction carries the weight —
     and it only works when someone is there to hear it.
   - Target sheet: business name, address, category, seed status, token state

## 4. The door workflow (seven beats, field-adapted)

Adapted from `CONSULTATIVE_CALL_SPEC` §2 — same beats, in person:

1. **Opener** — show their live listing on your phone: *"You're already on
   it — I want to make sure it's telling the truth."*
2. **Warm-up** — walk the NAP together; corrections saved live. They watch
   their listing get fixed.
3. **Build-up** — one sourced observation from the record (review strength,
   missing address on Google, parked website).
4. **Probe** — the call-side probes work better in person: storefront is
   *self-evident* standing in it; the pickup probe ("can a customer come
   pick up {product} today?") answers itself.
5. **Listen** — corrections, objections, hours, categories.
6. **Consult** — category selection on their listing (§2), products pitch.
7. **Pitch/close** — pioneer status (§1.1): founding cohort, product input,
   their shelf grows with the platform. Then claim *now*, on their phone.

**In-store claim:** mint a **bound** token to the owner's email/phone on the
spot → QR or text link → `/directory/claim/:token` → they register, the OTP
arrives while you stand there → claimed before you leave.

**Owner absent:** leave the **business card** (QR → platform root) +
storefront photo + note the manager-as-operator seed (§1.4) → unbound token
minted later → follow-up call within 48h. Generic QR caveat: the scan lands
on the homepage, not their listing — so **whoever takes the card gets told
what to do next**, said in person or written on the card/leave-behind:
*"Scan → tap Directory → search your business"* (seed exists) or *"Scan →
tap Directory → see who's already here — be a pioneer"* (no seed). The
card invites, direction + the follow-up call close the loop. Log the visit
as a touch (see §6 gaps).

## 5. What each door produces

| Outcome | Artifacts written |
|---|---|
| Claimed on the spot | `claimed` seed, `independent` tenant, storefront photo evidence, verified NAP |
| Interested, not now | Bound token minted, contact captured, touch logged |
| Owner absent | Photo, card, unbound token, follow-up scheduled |
| Refused | Touch logged — the funnel counts it; the seed stays live and indexed |
| Not a real storefront | Verification finding — flag for cleanup (`distinct`/`suppressed` paths) |

Every outcome is data. A refusal still leaves a live, indexed seed listing
— the door isn't wasted.

## 7. Post-visit pipeline

- Claimed pioneers → upgrade triad conversation on the *next* touch (not the
  same visit — let the claim settle).
- All pioneers → watch indexing: their `/place/{slug}` enters the sitemap
  automatically; the new category shelf goes live at first publish.
- Weekly: cohort board — doors knocked, decision-makers met, claims,
  shelves birthed, `G1–G4` funnel read.

## 8. Non-goals

- **Not autonomous.** Humans walk and talk; the platform pre-loads.
- **No paid conversion as the door goal.** The upgrade is a later touch.
- **No category engineering per pioneer.** The vocabulary absorbs new
  categories; nothing is built per door.
- **No invented content.** Provenance rules apply to field data too — a
  photographed storefront is sourced; a guessed hour is not.

## 9. Open decisions

1. **Pioneer terms** — the *frame* is decided: **initially free, growth
   partnership** (§1.1) — not a free product, not a promo window. Still
   open: how the growth path is stated on the door ("free to start, grow
   into it"? a named timeline?), what the pioneer *commits* to in return
   (feedback cadence — monthly check-in? open channel?), and what "product
   input" concretely routes to (a real surface — requests hub? a quarterly
   pioneer review?). Decide before door 1 — the pitch makes promises, and
   a promise with no machinery behind it is churn.
2. **Corridor choice** — which 2–3 Indy corridors (density of target
   categories vs. existing seed coverage).
3. **Field capture form** — build now or defer to post-cohort review.
