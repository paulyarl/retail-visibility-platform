# Pioneer Partnership Pitch — Field Script

Companion to `POS_INGESTION_PARTNER_PATH_SPEC.md`. Bracketed fields fill per merchant.
Keep it under 10 minutes unless they drive deeper — the demo sells, the talk frames it.

---

## 1. Opener — name, role, reason for visit (30 seconds)

> "Hi, I'm **[name]** — I work with local stores on getting their products visible where customers actually search. I'm not here to sell you a website or fix anything broken — your store works. I'm here because there's a spot on Google Maps where your products could be showing up that currently only the chains occupy. Can I show you on your phone? Takes about a minute."

**Why this opener works:** it disarms the "you're selling me something broken" reflex before it forms. Nothing to fix = nothing to defend.

## 2. What we currently know (pre-visit intel)

Walk in already knowing — shows you did the homework:

- Their store: **[Jokkymore African Market / pioneer name]**, **[address]**, **[rating★ / N reviews]** — an active, reviewed Google Business Profile (verified already = no Google-side verification battle)
- Their POS: **[NRS / Clover]** — meaning their catalog lives in the register but never reaches Google
- Their current Google presence: a customer can find the *store*, not the *shelf* — search a product they sell, no "in stock" answer from them, only the chains
- The gap they're about to see on their own phone (the demo below)

## 3. The demo — the phone in hand (60 seconds)

> "Open Google Maps. Search for a product you sell — **[specific item they stock]**. See who shows 'in stock': the chains. Now — same search, your store name: 'in stock near you,' your shelf, your product, two blocks away. That's what this is. Your customers are searching for things on your shelves right now and Google has no idea you have them."

If the pilot is already staged, show their **own store** live. If not, show the working demo storefront — same mechanics, honest about it being a demo.

## 4. What it actually is (the honest 30 seconds)

- Their POS already has the catalog — **[Clover: we connect to it directly / NRS: a simple end-of-day file upload]**
- We handle the Google side — a checklist with a few console steps we walk them through together
- **They choose which products** appear on Google — nothing goes live without their pick
- Their products show on Google Maps + Search **and** their own storefront — discoverable *and* purchasable

**The honest caveat (say it unprompted — it builds trust):** the "in stock" is only as good as their counts. If their stock data is right, this is a traffic driver; if it's stale, it sends customers for items that aren't there. We're selective about partners for exactly this reason — which also tells them *they* qualified.

## 5. The ask — pioneer terms (the micro commitments)

> "For pilot partners, this is free during the pilot — what I need is small: about an hour of setup together, and a few minutes a day keeping the upload habit. In return your store becomes one of the first independents showing 'in stock near you' — and I'd love to tell that story with your name on it, only if you're comfortable."

The asks, itemized:
- ~1 hour setup session (the checklist, done together)
- **[Clover: nothing else — sync is automatic] [NRS: the EOD upload ritual — a few minutes a day]**
- Products selected for Google (they pick — deny-by-default)
- Case-study permission in writing (name, storefront, results) — the partnership agreement covers it; no surprises in it
- Feedback — what works, what's confusing; pioneers shape the product

## 6. Objection handling

| They say | You say |
| --- | --- |
| "I'm too busy" | "Total ask is an hour once, then a few minutes a day — or with Clover, nothing daily at all." |
| "I'm not tech" | "That's the point — you upload a file or do nothing at all. We handle Google." |
| "What does it cost?" | "Free during pilot. Pioneer terms after that — and the first Google Maps customer probably pays for the month." |
| "I already have NRS/Clover" | "Keep it — this doesn't replace your register, it puts your register's data on Google." |
| "What if my stock counts aren't perfect?" | "Then we start with products you're confident about — you pick exactly which items appear." |

## 7. The close — one concrete next step

Never leave on "think about it" — leave on a step:

> "Two things to start: your Google Business Profile login — we'll do the link step right now, ten minutes — and a first catalog export from your register. When that's in, your products go live on the storefront today and on Google as soon as the checklist finishes."
