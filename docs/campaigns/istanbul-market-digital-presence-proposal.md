## 2. Business Model Hypothesis (Owner Validation Required)

The proposal assumes Istanbul Market wants to operate as a **hybrid Mediterranean & Middle Eastern market + cafe**, with two parallel revenue lines:

1. **Retail grocery / market products** — packaged goods, halal proteins, produce, bakery, Turkish/Mediterranean ingredients
2. **Prepared foods / cafe menu** — shawarma, döner, shish kebab, börek, halal hot food bar

### Owner validation questions
- Is the grocery side a meaningful revenue line, or mainly a support for the food concept?
- Does the store actively stock and sell market SKUs online, or is grocery in-store only?
- What fulfillment do they want? Pickup, delivery, or both?
- What is the target split between cafe orders and grocery sales?
- Do they want deposits/catering for large orders?
- Which brand name is preferred? “Istanbul Market,” “Istanbul Supermarket and Cafe,” or “Istanbul Market & Cafe”?

### Multi-category demo note

A seed can carry a primary category plus multiple secondary categories. The platform’s SEO composer currently caps at **6** secondary categories for metadata composition, and Google GBP allows **1 primary + up to 9 additional** categories. For the demo, the seed should be created with the hybrid category set — e.g. `Middle Eastern Restaurant & Market`, `Grocery Store`, `Cafe`, `Halal Restaurant`, `International Grocery`, `Mediterranean Restaurant` — so the operator can show presence across all category surfaces.

This is a configuration detail, not extra work: the seed is created once with the category set, and the same entry is demoed across each category.

## 6. Digital Experience Changes

### Website restructure
- **Home page:** hero communicates both market + cafe, not just “Order Online”
- **Navigation:** “Cafe Menu,” “Market,” “Catering,” “Order Online,” “Visit Us”
- **Product catalog:** two top-level categories
  - `Prepared Foods` — shawarma, döner, kebabs, börek, hot food bar
  - `Market` — packaged goods, halal, produce, pantry, bakery
- **Checkout payment per product:**
  - Cafe quick orders → full payment
  - Catering / large trays → deposit + balance at pickup/delivery
  - Market items → full payment, or deposit if special order
- **Fulfillment:** pickup and delivery toggles available across the catalog

### Google Business Profile
- Align categories with the chosen hybrid identity
- Add product posts for both grocery and cafe items
- Publish accurate hours and NAP
- Add photos representing both the market and the cafe

### Cross-platform cleanup
- Standardize brand name across GBP, Facebook, Yelp, Apple, Bing, MapQuest, Waze
- Resolve hours drift (9 AM–8 PM vs. 11 AM–7 PM variations)
- Consolidate website links to the primary domain or platform storefront

### Hybrid capability modules to consider
Because the business is a cafe + market hybrid, several platform capabilities become especially relevant once the core catalog and checkout are in place:

- **Styled QR codes** — table tents, receipts, packaging, and in-store signage linking directly to the storefront; track which channels drive reorders
- **Coupons and promotions** — cross-sell incentives like “$5 off a $30 market order with your next shawarma purchase” or “Free drink with a grocery pickup order”
- **Upsell / downsell funnels** — offer prepared food add-ons during grocery checkout (e.g., “Add a lamb shawarma for $12”) or pantry add-ons during cafe checkout (e.g., “Need Turkish tea or spices?”)
- **Social commerce surfaces** — Instagram shopping links, shareable product/menu cards, and “order this dish” deep links from posts
- **Presence surfaces** — directory entry, place page, GBP product posts, and map/platform syndication all pointing to one unified catalog
- **Loyalty / repeat-purchase nudges** — “Your usual order,” “restock your pantry,” and catering reminders for office/event customers

### Third-party citation / SEO signal note

The platform’s place entry is not just a display surface — it is a **third-party citation source** that search engines index. When the business’s NAP, categories, and brand identity are consistent on the platform’s directory/place pages, it creates a high-trust citation that search engines can use as a signal of legitimacy and relevance.

In practice, the platform acts as a **managed citation channel**: once the seed is claimed and the NAP is corrected, the place entry becomes an indexed, third-party proof point that reinforces the business’s local SEO authority across Google, Bing, and other maps/directory aggregators.

## 9. Suggested Operator Openers

The operator should pick the angle that best matches the owner’s stated priority. Each opener includes a hook, the business tension, and a natural bridge to the platform.

### Pre-call setup checklist

Before any outreach call, the operator should have the seed and demo assets ready:

- [ ] Campaign created for the business
- [ ] Directory presence seed created (tenant + listing + provenance)
- [ ] Seed published — place entry is live on the platform
- [ ] Public place URL noted: `https://{domain}/directory/{slug}`
- [ ] Claim URL noted: `https://{domain}/directory/claim/{token}`
- [ ] Screenshots or live view of the upgraded storefront / ordering flow ready
- [ ] One click-through path rehearsed: *place entry → claim → upgrade preview → storefront*
- [ ] The exact gap identified from the audit (hours drift, category mismatch, invisible grocery side, etc.)

This makes the demo personal — it is the business’s own listing, not a generic product tour.

### Angle 1: Brand mismatch / identity
**Opener:** “Hi, this is [Name] from [Company]. I was looking at Istanbul Market online and noticed something interesting — your name says *market*, your customers call you a supermarket, but your website opens like a restaurant. Are you running both a cafe and a grocery side?”
**Bridge:** “That’s actually a huge opportunity. Most places in Madison have to pick one identity online. We can build one storefront that does both.”

### Angle 2: Grocery invisibility
**Opener:** “Your shawarma and kebab menu is already showing up online, which is great. But when I search for Mediterranean ingredients, halal products, or Turkish groceries in Madison, the grocery half of your business is basically invisible. Is that intentional?”
**Bridge:** “We can list your market products the same way you list your dishes — pickup, delivery, and full payment.”

### Angle 3: Payment flexibility
**Opener:** “Right now it looks like every order on your site is paid in full at checkout. That works for a quick lunch, but for catering, party trays, or large special orders, are you able to take a deposit and collect the balance at pickup or delivery?”
**Bridge:** “We can set that up without changing your current website — deposits, full payment, and pickup or delivery, all in one place.”

### Angle 4: NAP / directory drift
**Opener:** “I noticed your hours and phone number are listed differently across Google, Waze, Apple Maps, and your own site. Some say 9 to 8, others show 11 to 7. How much of a headache is that for customers showing up or calling the wrong number?”
**Bridge:** “We fix the source of truth once and push it everywhere — plus clean up the categories so people find you for the right thing.”

### Angle 5: Catering / pre-order growth
**Opener:** “Your halal hot food bar and shawarma look like they could do well for office catering or events. How do you handle those bigger orders today — phone, in-person, or some other way?”
**Bridge:** “We can put those orders on the same storefront and let customers choose a deposit now, pay the rest at pickup, with delivery if they need it.”

### Angle 6: Competitive whitespace
**Opener:** “There isn’t really a Mediterranean market in Madison that also does fresh cafe food with real online ordering. You already have both sides. The question is whether the internet shows that clearly.”
**Bridge:** “That’s exactly what we fix — one digital identity for a market and a cafe, with every way your customers want to pay and pick up.”

### Business transformation openers

These frames are designed for an owner who already knows the business is more than a restaurant and is open to rethinking how the operation is sold online.

#### Angle 7: From menu page to full storefront
**Opener:** “Your current website is really a menu page with a checkout. That’s fine for takeout, but it doesn’t let someone buy spices, halal meat, or Turkish pantry items the way they buy your shawarma. What would it look like if your market and your cafe were both actually shoppable online?”
**Bridge:** “That’s not a website redesign — it’s turning your menu site into a product storefront, where the same checkout handles food, groceries, deposits, and delivery.”

#### Angle 8: Own the customer relationship
**Opener:** “Right now a lot of your online orders probably go through third-party menus or walk-ins, which means you don’t own the customer record. If someone orders a kebab today, do you have any way to bring them back next week for a grocery order or a catering tray?”
**Bridge:** “We’d give you one storefront with one customer list, so a lunch customer can become a market customer and a catering customer over time.”

#### Angle 9: Category ownership in Madison
**Opener:** “There isn’t a Mediterranean or Middle Eastern market in Madison that owns online ordering for both prepared food and grocery. You have both sides already — the kitchen and the shelves. The question is whether the internet shows you as the only true one-stop option.”
**Bridge:** “If we align your category, your catalog, and your checkout, you can own that search intent instead of splitting it with restaurants and generic grocers.”

#### Angle 10: Operational leverage, not extra work
**Opener:** “You’re already making döner and stocking Turkish ingredients. The work is already happening. The only missing piece is that both sides are not visible and sellable from the same place online. How much additional revenue could you capture if they were?”
**Bridge:** “We’re not adding products to your kitchen — we’re exposing what you already produce and sell, with payment and fulfillment options that match each type of order.”
