## 1. Market Summary

Madison's African grocery category is small and structurally so: the customer base is
diaspora-driven and Madison's African diaspora is modest relative to Milwaukee or
Minneapolis. Exhaustive discovery (SNAP registry mining, halal directories, the Afroblend
Wisconsin roster, press sweep, product-longtail queries, corridor walklists) verified
**5 qualifying independents** — one more than the curated Afroblend directory lists — plus
one closed location retained for identity archaeology. All 5 are inside Madison city
limits; adjacent cities (Sun Prairie, Fitchburg) produced zero qualifiers (restaurant and
multi-ethnic-supermarket mismatches only). The nearest verified African grocers outside
Madison are the Milwaukee corridor — reserved for a separate scale campaign.

**Why this campaign is worth running despite the small pool:** every qualifier fails
recommended gold-standard gates, 3 of 5 fail non-negotiable hours gates, 1 carries a false
"temporarily closed" flag, 4 of 5 have no Yelp presence, and 0 of 5 have a BBB profile.
Fixable-gap density per business is high, and the pool is compact enough for one operator
to work end-to-end with high touch.

## 3. Core Play: Status & Drift Rescue Call

The pitch: **call the business and verify operational status where data is missing or has
drifted.** The call converts "unverified" into sourced evidence — owner confirmation is an
allowed evidence type (including for SNAP, per the platform's SNAP evidence contract) and
upgrades provenance from "registry says / mirror says" to "owner confirmed, as_of date."

### 3.1 Standard verification checklist (every call)

1. Open status — checked against any platform status flags
2. Current weekly hours — ask about seasonal schedules explicitly (this category publishes them)
3. Address + suite/unit confirmation
4. Phone confirmation
5. SNAP/EBT acceptance — owner confirmation recorded with as_of date
6. Halal program — butcher counter / zabiha sourcing
7. Owner/manager name + preferred contact channel (WhatsApp is common in this segment)
8. Who controls the Google Business Profile and the Facebook page
9. Verbal OK to send a claim link

### 3.2 Per-store cards

#### ① African Market & AM Beauty Supply LLC — *Hook: "Half the internet still lists your old Gammon Road address."*

- **Drift:** SNAP registry, D&B, and MapQuest still show the pre-relocation address
  (805 S Gammon Rd Ste B); aggregator hours (Mon–Fri 5:00–7:30 PM, Sat/Sun closed) look
  stale; latest review evidence is April 2022.
- **Verify:** Seybold Rd Ste 224 is current; whether SNAP authorization transferred with
  the relocation; whether the evening-only hours are real; @africanmarketam / X accounts
  still active; owner (Benjamin Morris) contact.
- **Outcome:** NAP correction package (SNAP registry, D&B, MapQuest); GBP category
  correction — African goods primary, beauty supply secondary (the market's textbook
  INT_POSSIBLE_CATEGORY_MISALIGNMENT case).

#### ② East African Market: Halal — *Hook: "The government registry has you under a different name."*

- **Drift:** SNAP registers the address to "Halal And Hijab Market Inc." — rename, alias,
  or successor unknown; halal directories show garbled hours ("10:00 AM – 7:00 AM"); no
  website or Facebook page exists to verify against.
- **Verify:** trading name vs registry name (reconcile before writing any NAP record);
  real weekly hours; owner contact; halal program scope.
- **Outcome:** identity reconciliation record; first owned channel decision (Facebook
  page or directory-presence seed); claim invite. Textbook thin-footprint emerging prospect.

#### ③ Gooh Grocery — *Hook: "Google is telling customers you're temporarily closed."*

- **Drift:** aggregator/Google data carries a stale "TEMPORARILY CLOSED" flag while
  2025–2026 operation is corroborated (River Food Pantry 2025 participant, active Facebook
  content into 2026); website footer is ©2023 so seasonal hours (winter 9–7 / summer
  9–10) need currency confirmation; SNAP was pursued publicly but never verified.
- **Verify:** open status vs the flag; current season's hours; SNAP status; who manages
  the GBP; **Woo site state — who built/maintains it, is checkout live, any online
  orders** (site shows "under construction" category pages, a ©2023 footer, and a
  2022-era marketplace theme).
- **Outcome:** flag correction request; owner description (150–750 chars naming West
  African + Hmong + Middle Eastern + staples); review-generation plan (8+ reviews vs 10+

#### ④ African and American Store — *Hook: "Best reputation in the category, nearly invisible where new customers search."*

- **Drift:** ~18 years operating but no recent activity evidence captured (press features

- **Verify:** open status; current hours; Diallo family contact; status of the online
  shop referenced in press.
- **Outcome:** GBP claim; hours publish; hidden-trust → visibility plan (4.0 from 9
  reviews, ~188 check-ins — the deepest engagement signal in the market).

#### ⑤ International Market — *Hook: "Your Facebook page is your website — let's make it count."*

- **Drift:** minimal — freshest evidence in the set (46+ reviews through May 2025, daily

## 6. Phase 2: Post-Claim Nurture — the "Genuine Difference + What's Next" Call

**Timing:** ~2 weeks after claim acceptance (or after fixes go live for stores receiving
direct GBP/FB assistance without claiming).

**Framing:** the second touch confirms a genuine difference was made, then scopes what's
next. It never reopens the rescue frame and never leads with a tier menu — the platform
reveals tiers product-led (the claim accept response embeds the upgrade preview: three
Entry Presence mode cards, "from $X/mo" pricing, the Upgrade-to-Starter CTA). Phase 2
scopes that conversation to the gap chain documented in Phase 1 — each next step follows
from the store's own verified data, not a generic menu.

### 6.1 Call structure

1. **Confirm the difference (evidence-based):** name the exact Phase 1 fixes — "your
   hours now show correctly on Google" / "the closed flag is gone" / "you're listed
   under the right category now."
2. **Ask what they're seeing:** foot traffic, phone calls, customer questions since the
   fix. Listen for the difference being real. If a fix didn't land, that's an ops ticket,
   not a pitch moment.
3. **Bridge to what's next using the documented gap chain:** hours fixed → foot traffic
   → website next. The "what's next" is always the store's next gap, not the platform's
   next SKU.
4. **Scope the tier conversation to the trajectory** (table below); hand off to tier
   selection only where a real trajectory exists.

### 6.2 Per-store tier trajectories (anchored to Phase 1 verification)

| Store | Documented gap chain | Phase 2 tier conversation |
|---|---|---|
| Gooh Grocery | Stalled 2022-era WooCommerce build — "under construction" category pages, ©2023 footer, marketplace theme; **demonstrated ecom intent, unfinished execution** | **Ecom tier as Woo retirement:** managed storefront/checkout replaces the plugin-security and maintenance burden; mission-aligned hook (owners' stated SNAP-accessibility goal ↔ online ordering). Conditional — confirm the shop is stalled in Phase 1 before leading with it; pitch as "we migrate and retire it" (domain + email + SEO equity), never "abandon it" |
| African and American Store | Google Sites + referenced online shop; ~18 yrs of goodwill | Website tier to replace Google Sites — the strongest upgrade story in the set |
| African Market & AM Beauty Supply | Wix shop with a free-shipping threshold already live | Storefront / ecom-lite conversation |
| East African Market: Halal | Zero owned channel; SNAP-verified active retailer | Entry Presence → website as the cleanest first paid step |
| International Market | FB-only; 46+ reviews; actively renovating | Website / storefront — a habit-forming moment while the store is building |

### 6.3 Phase 2 guardrails

- All 5 stores have a trajectory, but Gooh's is **conditional**: lead with ecom only if
  Phase 1 confirms the Woo shop is stalled or unmaintained; if it turns out live and
  healthy, fall back to audit-fix value (a no-pitch store that got fixed is a referral
  asset, not a missed sale).
- If the owner raises tiers first, follow their lead; otherwise the tier mention comes
  only after the difference is confirmed.
- The free tier stays free-forever (`subscription_status = 'active'`, never trial) — the
  nurture call must never read as "the free thing was a teaser."
- Keep rescue framing out: the Phase 1 problem is fixed; this call confirms it and scopes
  the next gap.

## 8. Evidence Rules & Guardrails

- Every verification is recorded with an **as_of date** and source type, ranked:
  owner-confirmed > registry (SNAP) > press (dated) > directory mirror.
- **Never** infer SNAP/EBT from the "African" category label.
- **Never** record closure from a stale flag, an unanswered call, or a single directory —
  status changes require two-source or owner confirmation.
- Reconcile alias/relocation identity (registry name vs trading name, legacy vs current
  address) **before** writing any NAP correction.
- "Not found during discovery" ≠ "does not exist" — record both as separate observations
  naming the channel searched and the date.
- A thin digital footprint is not a quality, trust, or revenue signal — it measures
  digital maturity only.
- No competitive metrics in this campaign (no review-velocity comparisons, no leaderboard
  ranking) — competitive discovery is a separate campaign.
